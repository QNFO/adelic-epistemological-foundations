# The Adelic Physics Program — FAQ

**A compact Q&A guide to the QNFO Adelic Physics Program.**
*Based on: "The Adelic Physics Program: Epistemological Foundations and Communications Framework — A Synthesis and Survey" (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21686727)*

---

### Q: What is the Adelic Physics Program in one sentence?
**A:** It's the proposal that the physically accessible base field of physics is ℚ (the rational numbers), not ℝ (the real numbers). Since every measurement produces a rational number, and Ostrowski's theorem (a proven result from 1916) classifies all completions of ℚ, this makes the p-adic completions of ℚ physically meaningful — not mathematically exotic.

### Q: Why does anyone think ℝ is the base field of physics?
**A:** Because historically, calculus was developed over ℝ, and physics inherited its mathematical toolkit from calculus. It's an anthropological convention, not a physical necessity — no experiment has ever distinguished a real number from a rational approximation.

### Q: Isn't quantum mechanics formulated over ℂ, which contains ℝ?
**A:** The *formalism* is over ℂ, but the *physical content* is in measurement outcomes, which are rational. Using complex numbers in the formalism no more requires the reality of ℝ than using negative numbers in accounting requires anti-dollars. The map is not the territory. (That said, the same logic applies to p-adic completions — p-adic formalism means legitimate completions, not literal "walks on trees.")

### Q: What is Ostrowski's theorem, and why does it matter?
**A:** Ostrowski (1916) proved that every non-trivial absolute value on ℚ is either the standard Archimedean absolute value (completion → ℝ) or a p-adic absolute value (completion → ℚ_p). There is no third option. If ℚ is the physical base field, Ostrowski's theorem forces **all** these completions to be physically meaningful — not just the one we learned in calculus.

### Q: What concrete predictions does this make?
**A:** Most are still theoretical. The strongest current evidence is:
- **11 Standard Model mass ratios** fit Bruhat-Tits tree geometry to ~2% (Compton Cross-Ratios v2.3, DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21485556)
- **Zitterbewegung** — the Compton-scale oscillation of relativistic electrons — is interpreted as mixing between the ∞-place and 2-place (ZBW: Adelic Observable, DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21609223)
- **Room-temperature qubit** architecture proposed (RTAQ v2.0, DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21330960)

Independent experimental validation of all three remains an open challenge.

### Q: What does this have to do with the Gisin–Del Santo program?
**A:** Gisin independently argued that "real numbers are the hidden variables of classical mechanics" — the infinite digits of a real number encode all future states, making them indistinguishable from hidden variables. He and Del Santo proposed Finite Information Quantities (FIQs) as an alternative. This converges with the adelic program from a completely independent direction. See "Finite Specification, Ontological Indeterminism" (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21647362).

### Q: What is the Langlands Program doing in a physics paper?
**A:** The Langlands Program — a deep set of conjectures in number theory — connects to quantum field theory through S-duality (Kapustin & Witten, 2006). The adelic program interprets this as the statement of S-duality on the adele group G(𝔸)/G(ℚ). The FFT you already know turns out to be the Langlands correspondence for GL(1) in disguise (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21628383).

### Q: Isn't this just a massive self-citation exercise?
**A:** Honest answer: the 56 papers cited are all internal to the QNFO program. The three independent anchors are (1) Ostrowski's theorem (1916), (2) the Gisin–Del Santo program (external, convergent), and (3) the experimental observation of Zitterbewegung (Gerritsma et al., Nature 2010). The paper is explicitly scoped as a "synthesis and survey" — it does not claim to present independent experimental evidence.
